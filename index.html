What is StereoPep?

Existing peptide models achieve strong performance on natural-sequence proteomics benchmarks, but whether they capture underlying physicochemical principles or primarily exploit sequence statistics remains unclear. StereoPep is a benchmark of 48,788 chemically synthesized peptides spanning 11 libraries, produced via high-throughput flow synthesis and characterized by reverse-phase liquid chromatography retention time. Each library was tagged with optically pure L- or D-phenylalanine, generating matched diastereomeric pairs that share atomic composition and connectivity but differ in the stereochemistry of one or two residues.

🧬

Stereochemistry-Aware

8.3k matched D/L-Phe diastereomeric pairs isolate the effect of a stereochemical inversion from any change in composition or connectivity.

📊

Four Benchmark Tasks

Overall retention-time regression plus three structured pair tasks: diastereomeric pairs, single-residue substitutions, and terminal tag effects.

⚖️

Independent Test Set

The 2,726-peptide test set was independently designed and synthesized with no exact sequence overlap with training or validation data.

🔬

Experimental Data

All labels are wet-lab nLC-MS/MS measurements, normalized to %B (acetonitrile fraction at elution) for a consistent scale across gradients.

Key Finding

Across specialized retention-time predictors, molecular fingerprints, graph neural networks, sequence transformers, protein language models, and chemical language models, all approaches predict overall retention reasonably well (Pearson r up to 0.80). However, the predicted differences between matched diastereomers provide limited discrimination of which stereoisomer elutes later (ROC-AUC up to 0.61, where 0.50 is chance). Single-residue substitutions, by contrast, are predicted far more reliably (ROC-AUC up to 0.79). These findings reveal a substantial gap between overall property prediction and sensitivity to stereochemical differences.

A single curated dataset with experimentally measured retention times, partitioned into train / validation / test splits, with diastereomer pairs tracked separately. See the Dataset page for full details.
